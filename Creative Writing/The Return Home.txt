The Return Home

Three layers deep,
yet the cold still seeps through,
slipping past sleeves, whispering frost.

He steps from the elevator,
the scent of metal lingering in the air,
walking his scooter toward the light rail station.

The snow, indifferent to all,
falls relentlessly, endlessly.

He tilts his head skyward,
watching delicate snowflakes land on his glasses,
melting into cold streaks against his cheeks—
falling, falling, never ceasing.

A glance to the left,
the green-lit screen flickers its verdict:
H: Line Cancelled

He exhales, a fog of breath
drifting into the silence.

The wind bites harder now,
threads of ice slipping under his scarf.
His fingers, stiff in his gloves,
tighten around the scooter’s handle.

A train rumbles in the distance—
not his.

He waits anyway.

Because that’s what you do
when the world keeps snowing
and the night keeps coming,
and you still have somewhere to go.
